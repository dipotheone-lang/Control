# Internal Work Regulations — proposed amendment

**Closes:** open decision **O-06** (charter §12.3)
**Status:** `DRAFT — NOT LEGAL ADVICE.` For counsel to draft properly,

**Law:** Egypt. **The 2025 Labour Law replaced Law No. 12 of 2003**,

---

## 0. The verification that comes first

Everything below is marked `UNVERIFIED` in one respect: it assumes the
familiar structure in which an employer's Internal Work Regulations
define monitoring and disciplinary procedure, are filed with the labour
office, and bind employees who acknowledge them.

**That structure must be checked against the 2025 Labour Law before
anything here is drafted for filing.** The charter says so explicitly
at §12.3, and it is repeated here because the temptation to work from
pre-2025 practice is exactly the kind of stale assumption Control is
built to catch elsewhere.

Specifically, counsel should establish:

1. Whether IWR filing is still required, with whom, and on what
   timetable
2. Whether the 2025 Law changes what an employer must disclose before
   monitoring work communications
3. Whether transitional arrangements affect an IWR filed under the old
   law, and whether this amendment triggers a full refiling
4. Whether employee acknowledgement must take a particular form

---

## 1. Why the IWR must change at all

§12.3: Control's authority to monitor, and any use of its findings in a
disciplinary context, needs a basis in the Internal Work Regulations
and in employment contracts.

The practical risk is narrow and worth stating plainly. If Control's
findings are ever used to support a disciplinary decision, and the IWR
does not define the system, its scope, what it records, and the limits
on using its output, the employer is relying on evidence produced by a
monitoring system nobody was told about in the document that governs
monitoring. That is a weak position in a labour dispute, and the
weakness is avoidable with a paragraph.

The reverse is also true and less obvious: **an IWR clause that defines
Control's limits protects employees and employer alike.** A written
rule that findings may never be the sole basis for discipline is worth
more to everyone than an unwritten intention to the same effect.

## 2. Proposed clauses — substance, not final wording

Counsel to render these in the correct form and language. The Arabic is
the authoritative version of the final text (§4).

### Clause A — Definition and purpose

The company operates an automated compliance system ("Control") which
monitors deadlines for statutory filings, commercial obligations and
internal reports, checks submitted documents against approved forms,
and monitors response times to external correspondence.

Its purpose is to ensure the company meets its legal and contractual
obligations. **It is a control over the company's processes, not an
assessment of any employee.**

### Clause B — Scope of monitoring

Control reads the company's shared functional mailboxes:
`control@`, `sales@`, `procure@`, `info@`, `accounts@`, `hr@`, `hse@`
and `marketing@`, all `@ubcsis.com`.

**Control does not read the personal mailbox of any employee.** Subject
lines of mail in the HR mailbox are removed before storage. Contents of
documents marked client-confidential are not read at all.

### Clause C — What is recorded

Which report was submitted, by whom, when, against which deadline, and
whether it matched the approved form; whether external correspondence
received a reply within the applicable response time; registered
absence and delegation; and a tamper-evident record of every action the
system takes.

Retention is governed by the company's retention schedule.

### Clause D — Use of findings

Findings may inform coaching, training, process and form redesign, and
resourcing decisions.

**Findings from Control may not be the sole basis for any disciplinary
measure, any decision affecting pay, or any decision affecting the
continuation of employment.** Where a Control finding forms part of the
basis for such a decision, the underlying facts shall be established
independently, and the employee shall be shown the finding and given an
opportunity to respond before any decision is taken.

### Clause E — Right to contest

Any employee may contest a Control finding by replying to the relevant
message with the word `DISPUTE` or `اعتراض` on the first line.

The escalation of that item is suspended immediately. The CEO
adjudicates. **Contesting a finding shall not itself be a ground for
any adverse consequence.**

### Clause F — Absence

Where an employee is on registered leave, Control routes items to the
registered delegate and does not escalate against the absent employee.
Registration of leave and of delegates is an HR responsibility.

**An escalation arising because leave was not registered is a defect in
the registration process, not a matter attributable to the employee.**

### Clause G — Notification of change

Any material change to what Control monitors or records shall be
notified to employees in writing in advance, in both languages, and
this regulation amended accordingly.

## 5. Questions for counsel

1. Does the 2025 Labour Law still require IWR filing, and does this
   amendment trigger refiling?
2. Does the 2025 Law impose a specific disclosure or consultation step
   before monitoring work communications?
3. Is Clause D — findings never the sole basis for discipline —
   enforceable as drafted, and does it need to appear in individual
   contracts as well?
4. Is Clause E's protection against retaliation for contesting a
   finding sufficient as drafted?
5. Does a 12-person company have any exemption or simplified procedure
   under the new law?
6. Must the IWR state working hours explicitly, and does 09:00–17:00
   Sunday–Thursday require any filing or notice?
