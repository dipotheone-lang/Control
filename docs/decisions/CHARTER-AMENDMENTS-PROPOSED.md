## 1 — §18, which the execution order cites four times

The order says *"The charter (v4.2 + §18)"*, gives D-53 as *"Gap
closure engine active (§18)"*, and hangs B8, B9 and B10 on §18, §18.5
and §18.6. The charter ends at §17.

So three build changes have a name and no specification. What follows
is drafted from what the order itself says and from how the rest of the
charter is written. **Where I have inferred rather than read, the text
says so** — those are the paragraphs to correct first.

> ### 18. THE GAP CLOSURE ENGINE
>
> The system is useful from month one and more accurate every month
> after. That only happens if what it does not know is tracked as
> deliberately as what it does. A gap nobody counts is a gap that
> survives into the year after next.
>
> #### 18.1 Every gap is typed by who can close it
>
> A gap is an open item: a missing data point, an unanswered question,
> a `TO BE CONFIRMED` field, an unmatched reference in mail. Each one
> carries a **type**, and the type is not a severity — it is the answer
> to *who can close this*.
>
> | Type | Closable by | Example |
> |---|---|---|
> | **FACTUAL** | A named person looking something up | The registered address; the renewal dates on the certificates |
> | **BUILD** | Control being built further | The ETA exception detector; the event register |
> | **LEGAL** | A qualified outsider, and never the system | The twelve legal positions; the statutory deadlines |
>
> *(Inferred: the execution order §6 names FACTUAL and LEGAL. BUILD is
> proposed because neither fits "the detector is not built" — it is not
> a fact anyone can look up and not a position counsel can take, and
> filing it as FACTUAL puts it on the wrong person's list. §18.6's
> three-way split also needs a third thing to split. Correct the name
> if you prefer another.)*
>
> #### 18.2 The engine closes only what its type permits
>
> The closure engine may close a **FACTUAL** gap when the fact arrives
> and is recorded with its source. It may close a **BUILD** gap when
> the capability exists and its tests pass.
>
> **It may never close a LEGAL gap.** Not at any confidence, not after
> any period, not because the position has held without incident. A
> legal gap closes when a named qualified human answers it, and the
> system records who and when.
>
> #### 18.3 Nothing closes silently
>
> Every closure is logged with the evidence that closed it and the
> gap's type. A gap that disappears from the register without a closure
> record is a defect in the register, and the monthly self-audit (§13.3)
> reports the count.
>
> #### 18.4 Reopening
>
> A closed gap reopens when its evidence is withdrawn, corrected, or
> superseded. Reopening is automatic and needs no approval — it is a
> tightening (§14.1) and the safety spine permits those.
>
> #### 18.5 The provenance ladder
>
> Every value the system holds sits on one of four rungs. The rung says
> how much weight the value can carry, and **a value never crosses a
> type boundary by climbing.**
>
> | Rung | Meaning | Set by |
> |---|---|---|
> | 1 · `inferred` | Derived by Control from timestamps, filenames or patterns | Control |
> | 2 · `ceo_stated` | Stated by the CEO from knowledge, not from a document | The CEO, in writing |
> | 3 · `document_evidenced` | Corroborated by the company's own records | Control, from evidence, offered for approval |
> | 4 · `verified_by_advisor` / `verified_by_counsel` | Confirmed by a named qualified outsider | That person, recorded by name and date |
>
> **The boundary rule.** Rungs 1 to 3 are the company's own knowledge
> about itself. Rung 4 is somebody qualified saying what the law
> requires. **No amount of rung 3 evidence produces rung 4.** An
> archive showing twelve years of consistent filing says what the
> company did; it does not say what the law required, and the two are
> different facts that a single number would merge.
>
> Promotion to rung 4 without a named human is a stop condition
> (execution order §7). Promotion to rung 3 is proposed by Control with
> its evidence and applied by a human. Demotion needs no approval.
>
> #### 18.6 The coverage index, split three ways and never averaged
>
> Coverage is reported as **three separate percentages that are never
> combined into one:**
>
> 1. **Obligation coverage** — obligations tracked against obligations
>    known to exist
> 2. **Evidence coverage** — obligations whose values sit on rung 3 or
>    above, against all tracked obligations
> 3. **Legal coverage** — positions on rung 4, against all positions
>    that need one
>
> **An average across the three is prohibited.** Ninety per cent
> obligation coverage averaged with zero per cent legal coverage
> produces a comfortable sixty that describes nothing real and hides
> the only number that carries a fine. The execution order puts it
> directly: *"Legal coverage will read 0% and must stay visible at 0%.
> That is D-52 working, not failing."*
>
> Each percentage carries its denominator. A coverage figure whose
> denominator is unknown is reported as `NOT MEASURABLE`, never as
> 100%.
>
> #### 18.7 What the learning engine may do here
>
> Closing a FACTUAL or BUILD gap on recorded evidence is Tier A.
> Proposing a rung 3 promotion is Tier B. **Anything touching a LEGAL
> gap or rung 4 is Tier C** and is never applied by the system.

## 2 — §12, so Control does not halt at the Phase 2 gate

§12 opens: *"Phase 2 does not begin until every item is closed."* Its
items include O-06, O-07, O-08 and O-10, all requiring confirmation
**with counsel**. D-52 says Phase 2 proceeds on CEO-stated positions,
and §6 of the order records that no counsel is engaged.

Both cannot be executed. Control follows the order — but §5.6 halts on
a state the charter does not permit, and §12 is on the never-learnable
list, so only you can reconcile them.

Add to §12, immediately after the opening sentence:

> **Amended 19-Aug-2026 by decision D-52.** Phase 2 may begin on
> CEO-stated legal positions with the items of this section open,
> subject to mitigations M1–M5 and review on 18-Nov-2026. The
> acceptance is the CEO's and is recorded as such.
>
> Three things hold while it stands. Legal coverage is reported at its
> true figure and never averaged into another (§18.6) — 0% stays
> visible as 0%. Every management report states that the positions are
> CEO-stated and unverified, in both languages. And the §12.4 usage
> policy remains a hard precondition of the first live reminder, because
> it protects the people the system reports on rather than the company
> that runs it.

That last sentence is the one worth keeping if you shorten the rest.
D-52 accepts a legal risk on the company's own behalf; §12.4 is not
yours to waive on the staff's.
