# Golden set — the go-live gate

**Charter:** §13.1 · **Decision:** D-03 (CEO-only, unanchored)
**Schedule decided 16-Aug-2026:** batches of 10, one per week

---

## Why this is the gate and not a formality

Before Phase 2, the engine must produce **zero false
`RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` or `NOT_ACCEPTED` verdicts** on the set.

The reasoning at §13.1 is worth keeping in view while doing the work:

> A system that wrongly returns correct work loses authority
> permanently, and it only gets one chance to make that impression.

In a 12-person company, one wrongly rejected report from an automated
system that copies the CEO is enough for people to route around it. The
set is small, so it cannot certify a *rate* — that is Phase 2's job,
across 30 live days. What this set certifies is that the engine does
not confidently reject correct work.

## Why you don't see my answer first

D-03: I present each item without showing my verdict. You judge, then I
run the engine and report agreement, disagreement by disagreement, with
my diagnosis of each gap.

Anchoring you to my answer would produce a test I cannot fail, which is
not a test. It costs more of your time and it is the only version worth
running.

## The schedule

Four sittings of roughly 50 minutes, one per week. This runs in
parallel with the counsel work on O-06, O-07, O-08 and O-10, which will
take at least a month regardless — so the golden set is not the long
pole unless it stalls.

| Batch | Items | Target | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 | Week 1 | Spread across report types; a realistic mix of clean and defective |
| 2 | 10 | Week 2 | Different submitters, including the two vacancies' interim holder |
| 3 | 10 | Week 3 | Includes the **clause-mapping subsample** — see below |
| 4 | 10 | Week 4 | Includes the **bilingual equivalence sample** — see below |

**If a batch stalls beyond two weeks I raise it as a deployment
blocker** rather than waiting quietly (§13.1). That is not pressure; it
is the charter refusing to let Phase 1 drift while looking healthy.

## What each item looks like

- The document as submitted
- Its governing form
- The relevant manual clause
- A one-line worksheet: verdict, and if not accepted, which of C1–C7
  failed and why

Roughly 5–8 minutes per item.

## Two sub-tests inside the set

**Clause mapping (batch 3, at least 10 items).** For this subsample you
get the full manual and **no pre-selected clause**. Whether your chosen
clause matches mine is recorded as its own error rate.

The reason: me selecting the clause frames your judgement. §13.1 says
that framing gets measured rather than assumed away — so this
deliberately makes one part of the test harder for me.

**Bilingual equivalence (batch 4).** A sample of generated bilingual
notices checked for English–Arabic equivalence. The Arabic is the
authoritative text in a labour dispute (§4), so a discrepancy between
the two versions is a tested defect rather than a stated hope. Re-run
on any template change.

## Selection

Drawn to span every report type, every submitter, and a realistic
spread of good and defective work — **not a curated sample of clean
ones**. A set of well-formed submissions would test nothing worth
testing.

Selection happens after the obligation register is approved, because
the set is drawn from real submissions against real obligations.

## After the set

Control runs its engine against it and reports:

1. Agreement rate
2. Every disagreement, item by item
3. Its own diagnosis of each disagreement — which check, which rule
4. The clause-mapping error rate, separately
5. Bilingual equivalence defects, separately

Then the gate: **zero false returns**, false-positive opportunities
counted **per check** (each item exercises up to seven), not per
document.

## The set never stops growing

Every dispute upheld and every human-overridden verdict is added as a
new case with your ruling as the expected answer (§13.1, §14.3). The
same error cannot recur silently. This is also why the retention draft
proposes keeping the anonymised test case permanently while deleting
the record that names the submitter.

## Single-point dependency, stated plainly

With CEO-only assignment, Phase 1 cannot complete without your time.
That is a deliberate choice — D-03 — and its cost is that this is the
one gate nobody else can move.
